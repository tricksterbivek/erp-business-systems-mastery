# Accounting 01: Explain what changed

Started: 2026-10-04. Target: Levels 1 and 2, Understand and Explain.

## Objective

Explain how business events affect assets, liabilities, equity, and profit before moving to debit/credit postings or NetSuite records.

Use the [company model](../company/COMPANY.md) and [learning rules](../LEARNING_RULES.md). This is an isolated synthetic opening scenario for ABG-AU, not the group's historical balances.

## Starting concepts

- Assets are resources the business controls, such as cash.
- Liabilities are obligations the business owes to others.
- Equity is the residual interest: assets minus liabilities.
- Revenue and expenses affect profit and, through profit, equity. Owner contributions are separate from profit.
- The accounting equation is Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

An increase in cash does not, by itself, identify the reason for that increase. Classify the underlying event.

## Scenario and assumptions

All opening balances are zero. All amounts are AUD and belong to ABG-AU. The exercise uses accrual accounting. Amounts are whole dollars. Tax, FX, fees, interest, inventory, and intercompany transactions are excluded from this isolated scenario, not from the wider company model. The three events occur within one accounting period; a fiscal calendar and close policy are not needed for this attempt.

| Event | Facts |
| --- | --- |
| E01 | An owner transfers AUD 20,000 into the company's bank account as permanent share capital. It is not repayable. |
| E02 | A bank lends ABG-AU AUD 8,000 and transfers the funds into the same account. The principal remains repayable. |
| E03 | ABG-AU pays AUD 2,000 from that account for office rent covering only the current period. The premises have been used; nothing remains prepaid or unpaid. |

## Acceptance criteria

- Classify each event using its economic substance, not merely its cash movement.
- Keep capital contributions, borrowing, and operating performance distinct.
- Explain every change in plain language and keep the equation balanced after each event.
- Reconcile ending cash to the three bank movements and explain profit or loss independently.
- Complete the nine-step trace without inventing inventory movements or integration requirements.
