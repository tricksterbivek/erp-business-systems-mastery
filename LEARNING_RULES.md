# Learning rules

1. **Attempt first.** Before a complete worked solution, submit an attempt: a model, calculation, explanation, diagram, code, or a specific account of what you tried and where you became stuck. A blank request for an answer is not an attempt.
2. **Agree the task before solving it.** Establish the business scenario, assumptions, target assessment level, deliverable, and observable acceptance criteria. Do not include the answer in the brief.
3. **Use staged help.** Start with questions about the attempt, then a conceptual hint, then a small analogous example, then a partial correction. Offer a complete solution only after an attempt and an explicit learner request. If no attempt exists, help produce a first step instead.
4. **Protect independent assessment.** Codex and ChatGPT should not implement the assessed behavior before your attempt. Repository maintenance and non-assessed scaffolding are permitted. Log substantive assistance so assisted work is distinguishable from independent evidence.
5. **Explain before moving on.** After seeing a solution, explain the reasoning and solve a fresh variation independently. Seeing or copying an answer does not demonstrate mastery.
6. **Trace all nine steps.** Business event → Business process → ERP transaction → Accounting impact → Inventory impact → Reporting impact → Integration impact → Controls → Automation opportunities. Explain any non-applicable impact.
7. **Make evidence executable where useful.** Prefer models, synthetic transactions, code, tests, reconciliation totals, and editable diagrams over long summaries. Include failures, reversals, retries, and exceptions when the task calls for them.
8. **Keep the simulation coherent.** Use company identifiers and distinguish ownership, custody, legal entity, tax registration, transaction currency, and reporting currency. Record new assumptions before relying on them.
9. **Keep the lab small.** Add briefs, artifacts, and utilities when needed; avoid empty lesson catalogs, duplicated company facts, unnecessary frameworks, or dependencies without a concrete benefit.
10. **Stay generic first.** Establish business and accounting behavior before mapping to a particular ERP platform. Tax assumptions are simulation choices, not professional advice or claims of statutory completeness.
11. **Track evidence honestly.** Update progress with target level, attempt links, help received, feedback, and remaining gaps. Reassess with new scenarios when evidence is weak.
12. **Control scope.** Start modules only on learner request. Do not advance automatically, commit, push, or publish without authorization. The current task creates the foundation only.
